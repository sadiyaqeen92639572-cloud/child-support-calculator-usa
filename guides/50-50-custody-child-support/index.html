Does 50/50 Custody Mean No Child Support?

Splitting parenting time exactly in half feels like it should zero out child support — but in most states, it doesn't. Child support formulas start from each parent's income, not just the calendar.

Why 50/50 custody usually still means a payment

Most state guideline formulas (the "income shares" model used by roughly 40 states) prorate the total cost of raising a child between both parents based on income share, then adjust for parenting time. If one parent earns significantly more, that income gap generally survives the custody adjustment — the higher earner typically still owes something, even at exactly equal overnights.

What actually changes at 50/50

What an equal schedule usually does is reduce the amount owed compared to a sole-custody arrangement, not eliminate it. Many states apply a parenting-time credit, a shared-custody worksheet, or a cross-multiplication formula once both parents cross a certain overnight threshold — see our joint custody guide for how that threshold varies by state.

When 50/50 custody really can mean $0

It's genuinely possible for the number to come out to zero (or close to it) when both parents have similar incomes and similar custody time — the formulas are designed so a truly equal case, income and time both, produces a small or no transfer payment. The bigger the income gap, the less true that becomes.

FAQ

Is there a state where 50/50 custody always means no child support?

No. Even states with generous shared-custody credits still factor in each parent's income — a large income gap will typically still produce a support obligation.

How do I find my exact number?

Use your state's calculator on this site, entering your actual overnight count — every calculator that has a custody adjustment applies your state's specific formula automatically.
