How Health Insurance and Childcare Costs Affect Your Child Support Order

The base child support number many people picture — a percentage of income, or a schedule lookup — usually isn't the whole story. In most income-shares states, the cost of the child's health insurance premium and work-related childcare gets added on top, then split between the parents.

Why these costs are added separately

The base guideline amount is generally meant to cover a household's ordinary child-rearing costs (housing, food, clothing) at a given income level. Health insurance and childcare are treated as extraordinary or add-on expenses because they vary enormously between families and aren't well captured by an average, so most states add the actual dollar cost on top rather than baking in an estimate.

How the split usually works

Once the add-on costs are added to the base obligation, the combined total is typically prorated between the parents by income share — the same ratio used for the base amount — though a few states apply a different split specifically to add-ons.

What counts as "work-related" childcare

Most guidelines only add in childcare costs that are necessary for a parent to work, look for work, or attend school/training — not childcare for a parent's personal time. Only the child's own health insurance premium (or the child's portion of a family plan) typically counts, not the parent's own coverage.

FAQ

Does every state add these costs the same way?

No — the exact treatment (whether it's added at all, how it's split, what counts) varies by state and by formula model. Percentage-of-income states in particular sometimes don't factor these in the same way income-shares states do.

Where do I enter these in the calculator?

Most state calculators on this site that use the income-shares or Melson model include childcare cost and health insurance premium fields directly in the form.
