Retroactive Child Support: Can You Get Back Pay?

Most child support orders apply going forward from the date they're entered — but in specific circumstances, some states allow support to be ordered retroactively, covering a period before the order (or even before the case) existed.

When retroactive support commonly applies

The most common scenario is establishing paternity and a first support order for a child where no prior order existed — many states allow support to be ordered back to the child's birth, the date of filing, or the date the other parent was served, depending on the state's specific rule.

Retroactive modification is different and more limited

Changing an existing order retroactively (rather than establishing a first order) is generally much more restricted — most states only allow a modification to apply back to the date the modification petition was filed, not earlier, specifically because retroactively changing an already-due obligation creates real unfairness for the other parent.

Why the distinction matters

"Retroactive to the first order" and "retroactive modification of an existing order" are legally different questions with very different rules in most states — conflating them is a common source of confusion. If you're asking about back-owed support, it's worth being specific about which situation applies to your case.

FAQ

Can I get retroactive support for a child born years ago?

This depends heavily on your state's rule and the specifics of your case (paternity establishment date, when a case was filed) — consult a family law attorney for your state's exact limitation.

Can a modification be backdated before I filed?

In most states, no — modifications generally only apply from the filing date forward, not retroactively to before the petition was filed.
